## About Me

I paint the moment before a feeling finds its name.

My work belongs to abstract expressionism, a language with no figures and no stories to follow, only colour, gesture and weight. I trained at Concordia University in Montreal and I am largely self-taught, which means my real education has happened at the easel: in thousands of hours of looking, mixing, pouring, failing and starting again.

### Colour as Language

Colour is the first thing I reach for, and the last thing I let go of. In my paintings it replaces the subject entirely. A field of violet that dissolves into turquoise is not a sky or a sea; it is a change of mood, a held breath released. A wall of dusty lilac and mineral grey, broken by pale vertical bands, is not architecture; it is the quiet of a room after a long conversation.

I work with a screen-born palette, the vivid, luminous colour of the digital world, and translate it into the density of oil: magenta set against deep teal, electric cyan held inside a ring of smoky mauve, red glowing from beneath the surface like a thought that will not stay buried. The meeting of light and pigment is where my paintings live.

### Movement and Mixture

Nothing in my process is premeditated. Colours meet while still wet. Paint is poured, dragged, scraped and left to blend on its own terms. Some canvases move in long horizontal currents, like water seen from far above; others are built from hundreds of short, rhythmic strokes, a field of small gestures that together feel like rain, or a crowd, or a pulse. In others a single loop of line circles and doubles back on itself, red, blue and black overlapping until they hum.

Each mark is a record of the body that made it: the speed of the wrist, the pressure of the hand, the exact second I decided to stop. I think of every painting as a trace of time rather than an image of a place.

### Warm and Cool

Temperature is my way of building depth on a flat surface. Warm colours advance: red, orange and yellow carry urgency and tension. Cool colours recede: blue, green and purple open space, stillness and silence. When I place them side by side, the canvas begins to breathe. A cool void is held inside a restless frame; a warm glow trembles under a calm skin. I am less interested in resolving that tension than in letting it be felt.

### Texture and Touch

I want a painting to be felt before it is understood. Impasto builds in ridges that catch the light, the grain of raw canvas stays visible, and in the digital works I use detailed surface mapping so that shadow and highlight behave as they would on physical paint. A screen becomes a surface with a skin, and the eye is invited to touch.

### For a New Generation of Viewers

I make this work for people who live between the physical and the digital, who scroll through a thousand images before breakfast and still long for one that stops them. My paintings ask for no special knowledge and no fixed interpretation. They belong to anyone, in any city, in any language, who has felt something large and wordless and wanted to see it.

Stand in front of a canvas for a while. Let the colour work on you. What you feel is the meaning.
